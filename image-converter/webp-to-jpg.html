A WebP photo of about 3 MB usually becomes a JPG of 0.8–1.5 MB at 85% quality. JPG has no transparent pixels, so if your WebP has transparency, the see-through parts come out white.

Questions people ask

Why convert WebP to JPG at all?

WebP is a good format — smaller than JPG at the same quality — but many upload forms, exam portals, government sites and older programs still refuse it. JPG opens everywhere, so converting is the quick fix when something rejects your WebP.

Will the JPG look worse than the WebP?

A little. JPG is lossy, so a JPG made from a WebP is a second lossy generation. At 85% quality the difference is hard to see. If you need every pixel, convert to PNG instead — PNG is lossless.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser, on your own device. Nothing is uploaded, so private photos stay private.
