Why convert at all

JPG opens everywhere. Every phone, every computer, every upload form accepts it. It is the safest choice when you are not sure what the other side accepts. The price is lossy compression: a JPG made from a WebP or HEIC loses a little detail, though at 85% quality the difference is hard to see.

WebP is the smallest. A WebP is usually 25–35% smaller than the same photo as a JPG, which is why websites serve it. Keep WebP for the web; convert to JPG when a form, an exam portal or an old program asks for it.

HEIC is the iPhone problem. Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos as HEIC to save space. The photos are good; the format is the problem. If you keep running into it, change it at the source: Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible, and new photos will save as JPG. The photos already on your phone still need converting.

PNG is lossless. Converting to PNG keeps every pixel, which is what you want for screenshots, logos and anything you will edit again. The files are bigger — that is the trade.

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser, on your own device. Your photos are never sent to a server, so ID photos, certificates and private pictures stay private. You can check the network tab in your browser's developer tools — nothing leaves.

Yes. Drop the HEIC files here and get JPG or PNG back. The first HEIC conversion loads a small decoder (about 1.3 MB); after that each photo takes a second or two.

Will converting lose quality?

JPG is lossy, so a JPG made from a WebP or HEIC loses a little detail — at 85% quality the difference is hard to see. PNG is lossless, so PNG output keeps every pixel. Converting to WebP usually makes the file smaller.
