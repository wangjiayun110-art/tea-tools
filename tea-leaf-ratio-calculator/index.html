Tea Leaf Calculator

How much tea per cup? Pick your tea, tell the calculator how much water you have — or how much leaf — and it gives you the other half. No sign-up, no app.

Most weak or bitter cups are not bad tea. They are the wrong ratio: too little leaf and the cup tastes like warm water, too much and it turns harsh. A tea leaf calculator fixes the guesswork, and once you have brewed the same tea twice at the right ratio, you will not need it again.

Tea-to-water ratios at a glance

These are starting points, not rules. Your leaves, your water and your taste will move them a little, and that is normal. Change one thing at a time — leaf, time or temperature — so you know what fixed the cup.

Gongfu brewing uses more leaf and much shorter steeps — 20 to 45 seconds at first, adding about 10 seconds each round — because the same leaves are steeped several times. A mug uses less leaf and one long steep of 2 to 3 minutes.

Why the ratio matters more than the tea

A good tea brewed at the wrong ratio still tastes wrong, and a plain tea brewed at the right ratio often tastes fine. That is the whole point of a tea leaf calculator: it takes the one variable beginners get wrong most often and turns it into a number. Once the ratio is right, the other two variables — water temperature and steeping time — are easier to feel your way into. And if a cup does come out bitter, reach for less leaf or cooler water first. A shorter steep is the last thing to try, not the first.

Questions beginners ask

How much tea should I use per cup?

About 2 grams of loose leaf per 250 ml mug for green, white and yellow tea, about 3 grams for oolong and pu-erh, and 2 to 3 grams for black tea. In a small gongfu pot, use about 5 grams per 100 ml and steep briefly instead.

How many teaspoons is 2 grams of tea?

Roughly one generous teaspoon, but it varies: fluffy white tea weighs less per spoon, and tightly rolled oolong weighs more. A small kitchen scale is more reliable than a spoon, and it costs less than a bag of good tea.

Does the tea-to-water ratio change for gongfu brewing?

Yes. Gongfu brewing uses much more leaf and much less time: about 5 grams per 100 ml, with steeps of 20 to 45 seconds. The idea is many short infusions from the same leaves, instead of one long steep.

My tea tastes bitter. Should I use less leaf?

Usually yes, or cooler water. Bitterness most often comes from water that is too hot or a steep that ran too long, but too much leaf makes it worse. Try a little less leaf first, and keep the time and temperature the same, so you change one thing at a time.
